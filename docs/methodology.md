# Data Methodology

This document describes how every record `localis` ships is produced: which sources each dataset comes from, the rules that combine them, how the results were validated, and where they are known to be wrong. It is meant to be falsifiable. Anyone who finds a result they don't trust should be able to trace it to the rule that produced it here. The rules and evidence come first; how each rule was arrived at, including the failures that motivated it and the one-off checks that validated the results, is recorded separately in Discovery, at the end.

## Sources and licensing

`localis`'s code is MIT licensed. The data it ships is derived from the sources below and remains subject to their terms. `src/localis/data/NOTICE` ships with the data and attributes each source, the full license texts are in `LICENSES/` and in the wheel's metadata, and the package declares the combined license expression `MIT AND LGPL-2.1-or-later AND CC-BY-4.0 AND Unicode-3.0 AND CC0-1.0`.

ISO 3166-1, 3166-2, 3166-3, ISO 4217, ISO 639-3 and ISO 15924 data comes from Debian's [iso-codes](https://salsa.debian.org/iso-codes-team/iso-codes) project, licensed LGPL-2.1-or-later, at the current commit of its main branch, which takes ISO's changes as they're published, well ahead of iso-codes' releases. [GeoNames](https://www.geonames.org/) supplies `countryInfo.txt`, `admin1CodesASCII.txt`, `admin2Codes.txt`, `alternateNamesV2.txt` and `cities500.txt`, licensed CC BY 4.0, which requires attribution. [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/) supplies the subdivision crosswalk and country aliases under CC0. [Unicode CLDR](https://cldr.unicode.org/) supplies, under the Unicode License v3 and from the current commit of its `cldr-json` repository's main branch: `territoryInfo.json`, the source of each country's languages and used to choose which alternate-name languages subdivisions keep; its territory containment and English territory names, the source of the macroregions; `currencyData.json`, the source of each country's currencies; `languageData.json`, the source of each language's scripts; and its English script and language names (`scripts.json`, `languages.json`), their aliases.

Every pipeline run builds every dataset from its sources, and the result replaces the shipped data only when the whole pipeline succeeds and the new build's record counts reconcile, so the shipped datasets always come from one run. Each source file behind them is recorded in `ingest/<stage>/inputs/*.manifest.json`, committed alongside the data, by its URL and the SHA-256 of its contents (with the server's ETag, used only to skip re-downloading an unchanged file). A zip's entry is its extracted file's, under that file's name. A request that fails or a download cut off short of its stated length is retried with backoff, never recorded. The manifests are written with the data and never otherwise, so the commit a release was built from identifies its exact source files, byte for byte.

Wikidata has no ETag, so both Wikidata queries run on every run and their results are committed instead: the subdivision crosswalk as `ingest/subdivisions/inputs/wikidata_crosswalk.json` and the country names as `ingest/countries/inputs/wikidata_country_names.json`. Like the manifests, each is written only when the pipeline's build is promoted, so the files record the Wikidata state each release was built from. A failed or cut-off query is retried with backoff, and a result missing any committed key is queried again, the removal accepted only once a repeat is missing the same keys: a Wikidata edit repeats and a flaky response doesn't. Only the missing keys must repeat, since the query service's servers lag behind Wikidata by different amounts, so two results can differ elsewhere.

## Macroregions

Macroregions come from CLDR: its territory containment (`territoryContainment.json`) gives the hierarchy and its English territory names (`territories.json`) give the names. CLDR documents its containment as an extension of the UN M49 standard, but the two differ, and localis follows CLDR, not M49. Where M49 divides a sub-region into intermediate regions, CLDR places the intermediate regions directly under the region and keeps the sub-region as a grouping: Mexico is Americas → Central America, and M49's "Latin America and the Caribbean" (419) is CLDR's grouping "Latin America". CLDR also adds codes M49 doesn't have: Outlying Oceania (QO) among the subregions, and the European Union, Eurozone and United Nations groupings. CLDR was chosen over M49's own table because it is versioned, machine-readable and already a localis source.

The terms are the sources' own. CLDR calls the set macroregions, and M49 names its levels region and sub-region. "Continent" isn't used, since neither source uses it and CLDR's regions (Africa, Americas, Asia, Europe and Oceania) aren't the seven continents taught in schools.

Each macroregion ships with CLDR's code, its English name and one of three types. The regions are the children of World (001) in the containment tree, and the subregions are the children of each region, so every subregion has exactly one region as its parent. Codes are M49's numeric codes as zero-padded strings, or CLDR's letter codes: Oceania is "009", so `lookup(9)` doesn't find it.

The groupings are every entry CLDR marks as a grouping: North America (003), Latin America (419), Sub-Saharan Africa (202), the European Union (EU), the Eurozone (EZ) and the United Nations (UN). All of them are included, since leaving some of CLDR's data out would be an editorial choice. A grouping's parent is the region CLDR files it under, so North America and Latin America sit under Americas and Sub-Saharan Africa under Africa; the European Union, Eurozone and United Nations are filed under World, span regions, and have no parent. The first three list subregions and the other three list countries directly. Groupings overlap without nesting: North America and Latin America share Central America and the Caribbean, and CLDR doesn't record the Eurozone as part of the European Union.

The ingest fails rather than guesses if CLDR stops matching this shape, such as a region's child that isn't a subregion, a macroregion without an English name, or a territory placed in two subregions.

A country's macroregions are its path through CLDR's tree, its region and then its subregion, and separately the groupings it belongs to. All <stat key="data.macroregions.current_placed:int">250</stat> current countries sit in exactly one subregion, including Kosovo (`XK`), in Southern Europe, and Antarctica's path is Oceania → Outlying Oceania, CLDR's own placement. The ingest fails if CLDR leaves a current country unplaced. Historic countries take only CLDR's placements for withdrawn codes, which covers <stat key="data.macroregions.historic_placed:int">10</stat> of the <stat key="data.countries.historic:int">31</stat>; see [Known limitations](#known-limitations). The path and the groupings, in CLDR's order, are exposed as `Country.macroregions` and `Country.groupings`, and both are filterable with `countries.filter(macroregion=...)`, by name or code.

## Currencies

Currencies are ISO 4217's list as iso-codes publishes it: each code's `alpha3`, its `name`, and its `numeric` code as an integer (so "008" is 8). All <stat key="data.currencies.total:int">178</stat> codes ship, including the funds (Switzerland's WIR currencies, Bolivia's Mvdol), precious metals (XAU), the bond-market units, the IMF's special drawing right (XDR) and the testing and no-currency codes (XTS, XXX), since leaving some of ISO's list out would be an editorial choice.

Minor units (how many decimal places a currency uses) aren't shipped. iso-codes doesn't carry them. SIX, the ISO 4217 maintenance agency, publishes them, but its list carries no license and its site's terms of use reserve its content, so it isn't a source. CLDR's `fractions` give digits, but they are CLDR's formatting practice rather than ISO's minor units and differ for several currencies (CLDR uses 0 for the Afghan afghani and the Albanian lek, ISO 2).

A country's currencies come from CLDR's `currencyData.json`, which lists each territory's currencies with the dates each was in use and whether it is legal tender. A country takes the entries with no end date that CLDR doesn't mark as not legal tender, in CLDR's order. That leaves out every fund code, which CLDR marks as not legal tender, and every withdrawn currency, so <stat key="data.currencies.linked:int">153</stat> of the codes are some current country's legal tender. <stat key="data.currencies.multi_currency_countries:int">7</stat> countries have more than one, such as Panama (the balboa and the US dollar) and Zimbabwe (the Zimbabwe gold and the US dollar). Kosovo takes the euro, since CLDR keys its data by the same `XK` code localis uses, and <stat key="data.currencies.current_countries_without:int">1</stat> current country, Antarctica, has none. A CLDR currency that isn't in ISO 4217 is skipped and logged. The result is exposed as `Country.currencies` and is filterable with `countries.filter(currency=...)`, by name or alpha3.

Historic countries have no currencies; see [Known limitations](#known-limitations).

## Scripts

Scripts are ISO 15924's list as iso-codes publishes it: each code's `alpha4`, its `name` and its `numeric` code as an integer. All <stat key="data.scripts.total:int">226</stat> entries ship, including the special codes (inherited, mathematical notation, symbols, unwritten, undetermined and uncoded) and the two entries ISO lists for the private-use range, Qaaa "Reserved for private use (start)" and Qabx "(end)", which mark its ends rather than naming a script; localis wraps the list rather than judging its entries.

ISO's names often fold other names into parentheses ("Han (Hanzi, Kanji, Hanja)", "Devanagari (Nagari)"). CLDR's English script names (`scripts.json`) are added as aliases: every name CLDR gives a code, its alternate forms included ("Simplified Han" for Hans), joins the record with that code and is deduplicated against the ISO name; <stat key="data.scripts.with_aliases:int">69</stat> scripts gain at least one. A CLDR code that isn't in ISO 15924, such as Qaag, CLDR's private-use code for the Zawgyi encoding of Burmese, is skipped and logged.

Scripts relate to countries only through languages: a language lists its scripts, and a country's language entry names the script CLDR gives its status for (see [Languages](#languages)). Neither ISO 15924 nor CLDR maps scripts to countries directly; CLDR's likely-subtags data names one likely script per territory for defaulting an untagged locale (India's is Devanagari only), which is a default rather than an account of the scripts a country uses, so it isn't used.

## Languages

Languages are ISO 639-3's list as iso-codes publishes it, all <stat key="data.languages.total:int">7,923</stat> codes: individual languages, the <stat key="data.languages.macrolanguages:int">63</stat> macrolanguages (such as Arabic, Chinese and Malay, which group individual languages under one code) and the special codes (undetermined, multiple, no linguistic content, uncoded), whether living (<stat key="data.languages.living:int">7,078</stat>), extinct, historical or constructed. Each ships with its ISO 639-3 code as `alpha3`, its ISO 639-1 code as `alpha2` where it has one, its ISO 639-2/B code as `bibliographic` where that differs, and ISO's `name` and `inverted_name` ("Arabic, Algerian Saharan" for "Algerian Saharan Arabic"). iso-codes writes scope and type as single letters, which ship spelled out (`"individual"`, `"living"`). ISO 639-5 language families aren't records.

Every name CLDR's English language names (`languages.json`) give a tag whose language subtag resolves to a record is added as that record's alias, deduplicated against its ISO names: CLDR's own name where it differs ("Bangla" for Bengali, "Mapuche" for Mapudungun, "Old English" for "Old English (ca. 450-1100)"), its alternate forms ("Azeri", "Pushto") and its names for regional and script forms ("British English", "Swiss High German"), which name a form of the language rather than another language. CLDR's menu fragments are left out, since each is half of a label rather than a name (`ckb-menu-core` "Kurdish" and `ckb-menu-extension` "Central" make "Kurdish (Central)"). iso-codes' one common name, "Bangla", joins them. <stat key="data.languages.with_aliases:int">117</stat> languages gain at least one. CLDR's codes are BCP 47's, ISO 639-1 where one exists and otherwise ISO 639-3, so a code resolves by `alpha2` or `alpha3`; one that resolves to neither, such as `kro`, the ISO 639-5 code for the Kru language family, is skipped and logged.

A language's scripts come from CLDR's `languageData.json`, which lists each language's scripts under its code and, separately, its secondary ones. TR35, CLDR's specification, defines secondary: "If the language is not a modern language, or the script is not a modern script, then the `alt` attribute is set to secondary." So Arabic's Syriac is secondary, as is every script of Sanskrit, Coptic, Aramaic and seven other non-modern languages. Each script ships as a `LanguageScript`, the script's base form with `secondary` set by that rule, primary scripts first and each group in CLDR's order. CLDR covers <stat key="data.languages.with_scripts:int">811</stat> languages; the rest have no scripts. `languages.filter(script=...)` matches primary and secondary alike.

A country's languages come from CLDR's `territoryInfo.json`, which lists, per territory, languages with the share of the population using each and, for some, an official status: `official`, `official_regional` (official in part of the territory) or `de_facto_official` (official in practice, such as English in the United States), shipped as `"official"`, `"regional"` and `"de_facto"`. Every language with one of those statuses is listed as a `CountryLanguage`, the language's base form with its `status`, `population_percent` and `script`, ordered by population share; the share isn't a ranking CLDR claims, and shares overlap, since people use several languages (Switzerland: German 76%, English 45%, French 39%). Languages without a status aren't listed, and an unrecognized status fails the ingest.

CLDR states status and share for a language written in a particular script, and its tags say so: Hong Kong lists `zh` (Chinese in its default script) at 5% with no status and `zh_Hant` (Chinese in Traditional Han) at 95% as official, and Montenegro lists `sr` at 5% with no status and `sr_Latn` at 100% as official. Each tag is kept as its own entry, with the tag's script as `script`, rather than merged per language, which would need a rule CLDR doesn't give for combining statuses and shares. A language can therefore appear once per script, as Serbian does in Bosnia and Herzegovina and Azerbaijani in Azerbaijan. A tag without a script subtag has `script` `None`; see [Known limitations](#known-limitations). Every script a tag names is one CLDR's language data lists for that language, so a country's entry never contradicts the language's scripts.

<stat key="data.languages.country_entries:int">492</stat> entries are listed across current countries, <stat key="data.languages.country_entries_with_script:int">20</stat> of them with a script. Kosovo takes Albanian and Serbian, since CLDR keys its data by the same `XK` code localis uses, and <stat key="data.languages.current_countries_without:int">4</stat> current countries (Antarctica, Bouvet Island, Heard Island and the French Southern Territories) have none. Historic countries have none, for the same reason they have no currencies. `countries.filter(language=...)` matches a country by any of its entries, by the language's name or any of its ISO 639 codes.

## Countries

ISO 3166-1 is the base for all <stat key="data.countries.iso_current:int">249</stat> current ISO countries, and none of its values are altered: its codes (`alpha2`, `alpha3`, and `numeric` as an integer, so "076" is 76), its `name` and its `official_name` ship exactly as published, and `official_name` is `None` where ISO gives none. That is why South Korea's `name` is "Korea, Republic of" and Taiwan's is "Taiwan, Province of China". The shorter names in everyday use come from `common_name`, which is not part of ISO 3166-1 but an addition by the Debian iso-codes project that distributes it, shipped in its own field and attributed to it ("South Korea", "Iran", "Taiwan", "Bolivia"; `None` where iso-codes has none).

Every other alternate name is an alias, and every alias comes from a named source, GeoNames or Wikidata (below); localis adds no names of its own. Aliases only ever add to ISO's values, never replace them, so a country is always found by its ISO name, its iso-codes common name or any alias. Search and filters treat all three name fields as names.

The <stat key="data.countries.historic:int">31</stat> ISO 3166-3 withdrawn countries, such as Czechoslovakia, Serbia and Montenegro, and Southern Rhodesia, are loaded with a `historic` record holding their four-letter `alpha_4` withdrawal code, withdrawal date, and ISO's comment.

ISO has reused codes across different withdrawn countries over time: `CS` meant Czechoslovakia and later Serbia and Montenegro, numeric 891 belongs to both Yugoslavia and Serbia and Montenegro, and numeric 716 belongs to both Zimbabwe and Southern Rhodesia. A historic entry is therefore resolvable by `lookup()` only through its unique `alpha_4`, never its bare alpha2, alpha3 or numeric code, and historic entries are excluded from `filter()`, `search()`, iteration and `len()` unless `include_historic` is set.

GeoNames' `countryInfo.txt` is matched to ISO entries by alpha2, and to historic entries by their former alpha2; where ISO reused a code, it goes to the most recently withdrawn holder, the country GeoNames still lists under it (CS to Serbia and Montenegro, not Czechoslovakia). It contributes each country's `geonames_id`, plus its GeoNames name as an alias when that name differs from the ISO names. ISO stays authoritative for the name itself.

A GeoNames country with no ISO 3166-1 or 3166-3 counterpart is added as a country in its own right; the only such entry is Kosovo (`XK`), which has no ISO assignment. ISO 3166-2 still lists the same territory as subdivisions of Serbia, so it ships under both countries; see [Where ISO and GeoNames disagree](#where-iso-and-geonames-disagree).

Wikidata names are added last, from a query committed as described under [Sources and licensing](#sources-and-licensing): for every item with an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code (P297), its English label, its English alternative labels and its English short names (P1813, the source of abbreviations such as USA, DRC, ROK and UAE). Where several items carry the same code, the one with the most Wikipedia sitelinks is used, which picks the country of Cyprus over a same-named item and Antarctica over the Antarctic Treaty area.

Each name is filtered mechanically:

- A leading "the" is dropped (so "the UK" contributes "UK").
- Anything containing a digit, a slash or a symbol is rejected as an identifier rather than a name ("ISO 3166-1:BH", "+256", "Atlantic/Faroe", flag emoji).
- A name of three characters or fewer is kept only as an uppercase abbreviation that isn't itself an ISO alpha-2 or alpha-3 code, which keeps UK, DRC and ROC and drops language and domain codes such as "el" and "zaf".
- The item's own IOC (P984) and FIFA (P3441) codes are dropped, removing sports codes such as CHI, CRO and URU that Wikidata also lists as alternative labels.
- ISO 3166-2-shaped codes ("GB-GSY", "US-GU") are dropped.
- A name containing a letter outside Latin script is dropped as mislabelled, since only English names are requested.

What these rules can't identify is listed in `ingest/countries/inputs/name_blocklist.json`, with a reason for each entry: nicknames and slang ("Down Under", "Murica"), demonyms ("Swiss", "Thai"), misspellings and stray codes. It only ever removes names a source supplied; localis adds none.

Historic countries take the names of every item carrying their ISO 3166-3 alpha-4 code (P773), which all <stat key="data.countries.historic:int">31</stat> have, so a code ISO reused, such as CS for both Czechoslovakia and Serbia and Montenegro, doesn't leave its entries without names; Yugoslavia's YUCS takes both federations' items. An item that also holds a current country's alpha-2 is that country's own item, which kept its old code (Burma's on Myanmar, East Timor's on Timor-Leste, the French Southern and Antarctic Territories' on the French Southern Territories), so its names stay with the current country.

Finally, an alias that another current country also has as a name or alias is dropped from both, so it can't resolve to the wrong country: "Saint Martin" belonged to both Saint Martin (French part) and Sint Maarten. Historic entries don't count here, since they are hidden by default, so Timor-Leste keeps "East Timor".

Once every source is merged, aliases are deduplicated by a comparison key that treats spelling variants of one name as equal: accents, case and punctuation are ignored, common abbreviations are read as the word ("St." as Saint, "Rep." as Republic, "Dem." as Democratic, "Is." or "Isl." as Islands), "&" as "and", and the filler words "the" and "of" are skipped. Of each group of variants only the fullest written form is kept, so Saint Barthélemy's "St. Barts", "St barts" and "Saint Barts" ship as "Saint Barts", and any variant of a country's own name, official name or common name is dropped. The same key decides which aliases two countries share. Subdivision aliases are deduplicated the same way.

## Subdivisions

ISO 3166-2 is the authority for a subdivision's official name, type and administrative hierarchy. GeoNames is the authority for its stable numeric `geonames_id`, its `geonames_code` (GeoNames' own admin code string such as `US.CA`, not a FIPS code), and a large corpus of alternate names. Where both describe the same record, ISO's structure wins and GeoNames supplies enrichment. GeoNames alone supplies everything ISO 3166-2 doesn't list, which is most of the dataset, and the hierarchy cities attach through. [Where ISO and GeoNames disagree](#where-iso-and-geonames-disagree), below, describes what that split means in the shipped data.

### GeoNames subdivisions

The <stat key="data.subdivisions.geonames_total:int">51,508</stat> entries in `admin1CodesASCII.txt` and `admin2Codes.txt` are parsed first. Each takes its country from its `geonames_code` and its `admin_level` from the file it came from; a merged ISO counterpart's level replaces it downstream. A record left GeoNames-only takes its level from its parent instead, one below it, so it follows the same definition as ISO's levels: under a non-administrative grouping's twin (level 0) it is level 1, like Ireland's Fingal beside the ISO-merged County Meath, and under an ISO level-2 parent it is level 3, like the districts of Iraq's Kurdistan governorates.

Aliases come from GeoNames' `alternateNamesV2.txt`, filtered to English plus the official language or languages of the subdivision's country according to CLDR, and to names written in Latin script: a name with any letter from another script is dropped, while accented Latin forms such as "Minskaja voblasć" are kept. Names flagged historic or colloquial, non-name entries such as links and postal codes, and duplicates are excluded, and invisible bidirectional control characters are stripped. Enrichment happens before matching, so the extra name variants also take part in fuzzy comparison.

### ISO subdivisions

Some ISO names carry trailing bracketed or parenthetical content, which is one of three things: a genuine alternate name (`"Girona [Gerona]"`), a bare subdivision code (`"Stockholms län [SE-01]"`), or a territory-dispute or type annotation (`"Aousserd (EH)"`, `"Amānat al 'Āşimah [city]"`). The content is split off at load time. A genuine alternate name becomes an alias and everything else is discarded, so it never pollutes matching.

`admin_level` is computed from ISO's own parent chain: 1 plus the number of administrative ancestors above the entry. Nothing caps the depth. The current data spans levels 0 to 3, where 1 is a top-level division, 2 a second-level division such as a county or district, 3 the deepest nesting observed so far (Bas-Rhin and Haut-Rhin, départements under the European Collectivity of Alsace under Grand Est, and the GeoNames districts under Iraq's Kurdistan governorates and Equatorial Guinea's provinces), and 0 a non-administrative grouping.

A grouping is non-administrative only if it passes two tests: structurally it exists only to group real divisions, and it has no administrative function at all, verified independently. Currently <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.countries:int">9</stat> countries qualify:

- Indonesia groups its <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.ID.children:int">38</stat> provinces under <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.ID.groupings:int">7</stat> "Geographical unit" entries.
- The Dominican Republic groups its <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.DO.children_by_type.Province:int">31</stat> provinces and the Distrito Nacional under <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.DO.groupings:int">10</stat> planning "Region" entries.
- Cabo Verde groups its <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.CV.children:int">22</stat> municipalities under <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.CV.groupings:int">2</stat> "Geographical region" island groups.
- Ireland groups its <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.IE.children:int">26</stat> counties under <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.IE.groupings:int">4</stat> ceremonial "Province" entries.
- Iceland groups its <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.IS.children:int">64</stat> municipalities under <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.IS.groupings:int">8</stat> statistical "Region" entries.
- Lithuania groups its <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.LT.children:int">60</stat> municipalities under <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.LT.groupings:int">10</stat> "County" entries that have had no governing authority since 2010.
- Malawi groups its <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.MW.children:int">28</stat> districts under <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.MW.groupings:int">3</stat> statistical "Region" entries.
- Guinea-Bissau groups its <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.GW.children:int">8</stat> regions under <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.GW.groupings:int">3</stat> "Province" entries (Leste, Norte, Sul) that have no political or administrative effect.
- The Marshall Islands group their <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.MH.children:int">24</stat> municipalities under <stat key="data.subdivisions.non_administrative.MH.groupings:int">2</stat> "Chain (of islands)" entries, Ralik and Ratak, which are geographic rather than administrative.

Groupings that look structurally identical but have real administrative function do not qualify: Iraq's Kurdistan Region and the Philippines' regions, which have Regional Development Councils and national agency field operations, are both treated as real levels.

The rule is stored by country and type in `NON_ADMINISTRATIVE_TYPES` in `ingest/subdivisions/outputs/resolution_map.json`, so it covers new matching entries automatically. A matching entry gets `admin_level=0`, keeps its real ISO `parent` reference, and skips name matching. Its children compute their own levels as if it weren't there.

### Merge precedence

`resolution_map.json` is the single record of how every ISO subdivision was resolved. Resolutions apply in a fixed order: resolve-subdivisions skill decisions first, then the Wikidata crosswalk, then the non-administrative bypass, then automerge for whatever remains. An earlier stage's decision can never lose its GeoNames target to a later one, because the later stage never sees that subdivision.

The bypass applies the non-administrative rule described under [ISO subdivisions](#iso-subdivisions). Some groupings do have a GeoNames record, because GeoNames keeps the tier as its level 1: Lithuania's counties, Iceland's regions, Ireland's provinces and Malawi's regions. Such a grouping merges into that record, its twin, found structurally rather than by name: the twin is the GeoNames level-1 record that every one of the grouping's already-merged children sits under. Name matching would be unsafe here, since Indonesia's "Papua" grouping shares its name with Papua province. A grouping whose children merged into level-1 records has no twin and is added as-is.

The bypass runs after the Wikidata step and before automerge, so the twins are claimed before automerge can match a child to one: it had merged the Utena district municipality (`LT-54`) into Utena County's level-1 record, because bypassing the counties puts Lithuania's municipalities at level 1, where automerge's pool was the county records. The twin can only be found from children merged by skill decisions or Wikidata, so if it isn't found in time, a check after automerge catches any merge of a child into a level-1 record its merged siblings sit beneath and sends it to review as a `grouping_twin` orphan.

A skill decision outranks Wikidata, but it can't overrule it silently. Every run compares each skill decision with Wikidata's valid mapping for the same code. A decision that disagrees and wasn't made with that mapping in view (each decision records the Wikidata mapping that existed when it was made) goes back to the skill as a `wikidata_conflict` orphan. The skill either confirms Wikidata or keeps the decision with a stated reason why Wikidata is wrong, and a later change on Wikidata's side reopens it. The `RU-AL`/`RU-ALT` swap survived for weeks because an old skill decision quietly outranked a correct Wikidata mapping; this rule exists so that can't happen again.

Wikidata can't relink a subdivision silently either. A mapping isn't applied if it would change what a code previously resolved to: the committed crosswalk's mapping or, for a code Wikidata didn't map, automerge's match. It goes to the skill as a `wikidata_changed` orphan carrying both records, and stays one across reruns until the skill decides. A code's first mapping applies unreviewed, and a removed mapping falls through to automerge. A change isn't accepted because automerge's scoring agrees with it: every relink of a shipped subdivision is reviewed.

A subdivision the pipeline can't resolve unreviewed is an orphan, sorted into one of seven buckets by why:

- `ambiguity`: several close GeoNames candidates.
- `low_margin`: a qualifying match too close to its threshold to accept unreviewed.
- `no_candidates`: none at all.
- `no_matches`: candidates exist but none qualifies.
- `wikidata_conflict`: a skill decision sent back for disagreeing with Wikidata.
- `wikidata_changed`: a Wikidata mapping that changed a previous resolution.
- `grouping_twin`: an automerge result that merged a subdivision into the GeoNames record of the non-administrative grouping it belongs to, detected because the subdivision's merged siblings sit beneath that record.

If any orphan remains, ingest exits with code 10 before writing any data, so the shipped dataset never contains an unresolved subdivision. Orphans are resolved with the resolve-subdivisions skill.

Wikidata and automerge results are recomputed on every run. A correction to either always goes through a skill decision, the only resolution that overrides them. No per-entry audit record exists yet: an earlier `audited` record, which was never populated, was removed, because the planned audit system binds each verdict to the facts it was made against and keeps superseded verdicts rather than deleting them, which that record's design couldn't do.

### The resolve-subdivisions skill

The resolve-subdivisions skill is an AI agent, run through Claude Code, that resolves orphans. It requires Opus 5.5 or stronger, since earlier runs with Sonnet 5 made wrong merges and unjustified adds; see [Discovery](#discovery) for the audit of its Opus 5.5 decisions.

It works under fixed constraints. It can only choose a GeoNames candidate supplied by the pipeline, and it can never invent an ID. Candidates the type-family rule would reject are shown to it, marked as a type mismatch, rather than hidden, because the rule's naming signal is sometimes wrong: Hamburg is both a city and a German state, so its GeoNames record reads as a city while ISO types it a Land. Candidates the directional guard rejects are shown the same way, marked, because a directional word can be part of another name for the same place: ISO's "Southern Grenadine Islands" is GeoNames' "Carriacou and Petite Martinique". It cannot merge into a record another ISO subdivision already holds. If nothing in its top-ranked candidates fits, it runs a web search to learn the place's other names, parent region and level before checking the rest.

It can declare that an orphan has no GeoNames counterpart only for one of four stated reasons:

- GeoNames uses a different administrative scheme for the country (Madagascar's regions against ISO's provinces, London's boroughs below GeoNames' second level, cities with their own administrative status such as Hungary's cities with county rights and Thailand's Pattaya, which GeoNames records only as populated places inside a larger district, Monaco's quarters, where GeoNames has a single record for the whole municipality).
- GeoNames treats the place as its own country (France's overseas departments, the US outlying areas).
- A real merger or split retired the subdivision and GeoNames has no record of it.
- The land is disputed and filed under another country in GeoNames (Serbia's Kosovo districts, which as a result ship alongside Kosovo's own GeoNames subdivisions; see [Where ISO and GeoNames disagree](#where-iso-and-geonames-disagree)).

A place merely too new for GeoNames doesn't qualify, since a "no counterpart" decision is permanent and would become a duplicate once GeoNames adds it.

Every merge also needs a one-sentence reason saying why the two records are the same place. The skill's rules live in its own instructions and nowhere else; the agent may not apply rules from memory.

Everything else goes to a human: a match claimed by another ISO code, several equally plausible candidates, a place that maps to only part of a GeoNames record or to several, and any unexplained absence. The human then decides to merge it or add it as-is.

Every outcome is written to `skill_decisions` with its reason and whether the agent or a human made it, and a human decision also keeps the agent's findings that prompted the escalation. A decision stays permanent until a human revisits it. A decision without a recorded reason and decider isn't kept: every decision has to be traceable to the evidence behind it.

The agent's candidates are every GeoNames subdivision in the orphan's country, sorted by name similarity with same-level candidates first on ties. Unlike automerge, it is not limited to the orphan's own level, because ISO and GeoNames sometimes disagree on a subdivision's level: Tainan is a level-1 special municipality in ISO but a level-2 record under Taiwan Province in GeoNames. Records already held by another ISO subdivision are shown and marked, so a wrong earlier claim is visible and escalated rather than silently worked around. An orphan in any bucket but `no_candidates` and `no_matches` sees the specific records the pipeline flagged first: automerge's contested targets or its pick, or both sides of a Wikidata conflict or change. Automerge stays strict and unsupervised while the reviewed stage gets the wider view, consistent with preferring an unmerged record over a false merge.

### Wikidata crosswalk

Wikidata is queried for every item carrying both an ISO 3166-2 code (P300) and a GeoNames ID (P1566), keeping only codes that map to exactly one GeoNames ID; <stat key="data.wikidata.crosswalk_codes:int">4,968</stat> codes qualify. A match is applied only if that ID exists in localis's GeoNames admin data and belongs to the same country as the ISO code. Anything else falls through to automerge.

A removed mapping falls through to automerge unreviewed, which is why a result missing committed codes is confirmed by a repeat query before it's kept (see [Sources and licensing](#sources-and-licensing)). A confirmed removal is accepted as Wikidata's and listed in the ingest log, where the ingest PR review sees it beside the crosswalk's diff.

Most remaining disagreements with the rest of the pipeline come from one GeoNames pattern: a place often has two GeoNames records, an administrative-boundary record (the kind in `admin1CodesASCII.txt` and `admin2Codes.txt`) and a populated-place record, and Wikidata's P1566 sometimes points at the populated-place one. Such an ID doesn't exist in localis's admin data, so the validation above already rejects it. That pattern accounts for <stat key="data.wikidata.disagreements_not_admin:int">276</stat> of the <stat key="data.wikidata.disagreements:int">278</stat> disagreements, and it holds for every Hungarian city with county rights and every Marshall Islands municipality.

### Automerge

An ISO subdivision whose country has no GeoNames subdivisions at all, such as Singapore, has nothing to merge with or review, so automerge adds it as-is and records it in `automerge.geonames_absent`. Because automerge recomputes every run, it merges automatically if GeoNames ever adds records for that country, which a permanent skill decision would block.

Automerge compares each remaining ISO subdivision only with unclaimed GeoNames subdivisions of the same country and administrative level. Level 3 is grouped with level 2, since GeoNames never nests deeper than 2. A pair must clear every gate below, in order.

#### 1. Type-family disqualification

A pair is rejected before scoring if GeoNames' raw, unstripped name or aliases carry a qualifier word from a different family than the ISO side's `type`. There are deliberately only two families: `city` (city, shi, si, gorodskoy, town, and equivalents) and `area` (everything else). This stops a city from taking its surrounding region's record on string similarity alone. `UA-30` Kyiv, the city, otherwise matches GeoNames' `UA.13`, which is Kyiv Oblast. Finer families such as province against county are not used, because sources describe the same tier with different translated words.

#### 2. Name normalization and noise-token stripping

Both sides' names and aliases are casefolded, stripped of accents, with Latin letters that have no Unicode decomposition spelled out (ß, æ, ø, ł and ı as ss, ae, o, l and i), and stripped of administrative qualifier words (`NOISE_TOKENS`), so "Province" on one side and nothing on the other doesn't cost a match. The token list spans dozens of languages and was built by counting the recurring leading and trailing words in GeoNames' raw admin names.

#### 3. Fuzzy match scoring

Pairs are scored with `token_sort_ratio` against a threshold of 90, lowered by 15 for names averaging five characters or fewer, by 10 for eight or fewer, and by 5 for twelve or fewer, since a single edit matters more in a short name. `token_sort_ratio` is used instead of `token_set_ratio` because the latter rates a full token subset as a near-perfect match, which is wrong when the extra words name a different place: "Val-de-Marne" is not "Marne".

A separate check vetoes any pair that differs only by a directional word such as north, south, east, west, upper, lower or central. Where a false merge and an unmerged record trade off, the threshold favors leaving the record unmerged, since a false merge ships silently as fact while an unmerged record is visible and recoverable.

#### 4. Ambiguity threshold

Before any assignment, a GeoNames target that two or more different ISO subdivisions each score 90 or higher against is excluded from automerge. This catches namesake collisions, most often a city and its surrounding district sharing a bare name, where string similarity cannot break the tie. Those ISO subdivisions become `ambiguity` orphans.

#### 5. Global best-score assignment

Remaining pairs are claimed in descending score order across the whole bucket, so a strong match is never blocked by a weaker one claimed earlier in iteration.

#### 6. Margin floor

A pair that wins assignment but scores less than 5 points over its threshold is not merged. It becomes a `low_margin` orphan carrying that candidate, so the skill can confirm or reject the specific pair. Every confirmed false positive found so far sat within 3 points of its threshold, while a correct near-threshold match such as `PE-CAL` El Callao to Callao is only delayed for one review, not lost.

### Where ISO and GeoNames disagree

The two sources split authority rather than one being the backbone of the other. ISO 3166-2 is authoritative for the identity and attributes of everything it lists: every one of its <stat key="data.subdivisions.iso_total:int">5,046</stat> codes ships, and a merged record takes ISO's name, type, level and parent, with GeoNames' name kept as an alias.

GeoNames is the backbone for coverage and for cities: <stat key="data.subdivisions.geonames_only:int">46,665</stat> of the <stat key="data.subdivisions.total:int">51,711</stat> subdivisions exist only in GeoNames, mostly its second level, which ISO 3166-2 doesn't list, and cities attach to subdivisions through GeoNames' admin codes, so a city's chain follows GeoNames' hierarchy and an ISO-only record can never appear in it. The non-administrative rule is the one place localis overrides ISO's structure itself.

When the two describe a place differently and no record can be merged with the other, both ship side by side. Each record is correct for its source; what a user sees is the same territory described twice. There are three ways this happens.

**Disputed territory: Kosovo.** ISO 3166-2 lists the territory under Serbia: the autonomous province of Kosovo-Metohija (`RS-KM`) and its <stat key="data.subdivisions.kosovo.serbian_okrugs:int">5</stat> districts (`RS-25` Kosovski okrug, `RS-26` Pećki, `RS-27` Prizrenski, `RS-28` Kosovsko-Mitrovački, `RS-29` Kosovsko-Pomoravski). GeoNames files the same land under Kosovo (`XK`), which localis includes as a country because GeoNames does (see [Countries](#countries)), with its own <stat key="data.subdivisions.kosovo.districts:int">7</stat> districts (Ferizaj, Gjakova, Gjilan, Mitrovica, Pec, Pristina, Prizren) and their <stat key="data.subdivisions.kosovo.municipalities:int">38</stat> municipalities. <stat key="data.subdivisions.kosovo.serbian_okrugs:int">5</stat> okrugs don't correspond to <stat key="data.subdivisions.kosovo.districts:int">7</stat> districts, so the <stat key="data.subdivisions.kosovo.serbian_records:int">6</stat> Serbian records ship as ISO-only records and the <stat key="data.subdivisions.kosovo.kosovan_records:int">45</stat> Kosovan records as GeoNames-only records. For a user this means:

- `subdivisions.filter(country="RS")` includes Kosovo-Metohija and its <stat key="data.subdivisions.kosovo.serbian_okrugs:int">5</stat> okrugs, while `subdivisions.filter(country="XK")` returns Kosovo's districts and municipalities for the same territory.
- A search such as "Prizren" can return both Serbia's Prizrenski okrug and Kosovo's Prizren district.
- Every city in the territory belongs to Kosovo and its districts; none can belong to the Serbian records, which have no GeoNames admin code.

The Serbian records are kept deliberately: the resolve-subdivisions skill's rule for disputed territory adds ISO's entries as ISO lists them, without taking a side. That neutrality is partial, since at the country and city level localis follows GeoNames, and Serbia's claim survives only in its ISO 3166-2 subdivisions. Dropping either set would be a policy decision rather than a data fix, departing from either ISO 3166-2 or the country and city layers.

**Different administrative schemes.** Where ISO and GeoNames divide a country into different units, ISO's unit ships as an ISO-only record beside GeoNames' units for the same land. County Dublin (`IE-D`) sits beside the four councils GeoNames divides it into (Dublin City, Fingal, South Dublin, Dún Laoghaire–Rathdown), all at level 1, since County Dublin has had no council since 1994. Monaco's ISO quarters sit beside GeoNames' single record for the whole Municipality of Monaco. The Métropole de Lyon (`FR-69M`) overlaps the Rhône department record (`FR-69`), which GeoNames still draws to include it, and the European Collectivity of Alsace (`FR-6AE`) overlaps Bas-Rhin and Haut-Rhin.

**ISO's update lag.** ISO 3166-2 is authoritative, so a unit it still lists ships even after a reorganization has replaced it, beside GeoNames' successors. Norway's 2020 counties Viken, Vestfold og Telemark and Troms og Finnmark ship as ISO-only records next to GeoNames' records for the counties they were split back into in 2024 (Akershus, Buskerud, Østfold, Vestfold, Telemark, Troms and Finnmark). Vietnam's provinces absorbed in its 2025 reform ship as ISO-only records, while the province that kept each new unit's name merges into GeoNames' enlarged record, so an absorbed province's land is covered both by its own record and by its successor's. Angola's Cuando Cubango, split in 2024, and Ethiopia's Southern Nations, Nationalities and Peoples' Region, dissolved in 2023, ship beside their successors the same way. These resolve themselves when ISO catches up: a code ISO withdraws drops out of the next ingest.

## Cities

Cities come from GeoNames' `cities500.txt`, GeoNames' own export of every populated place with a population of 500 or more, plus administrative seats of any size. No further population or feature filtering is applied. A city without a country code, or whose country isn't in localis's country data, is dropped and logged. A missing population is recorded as 0.

A city's country is attached by its ISO alpha2 code. Its subdivisions are attached through its GeoNames admin1 and admin2 codes: the deepest one that resolves is taken, and its parent chain is walked to the top, so `City.subdivisions` holds the city's full administrative chain in ascending `admin_level` order. That includes ISO levels GeoNames itself doesn't have, such as France's third level or a non-administrative grouping. A city can only link to a subdivision that has a GeoNames code, so the <stat key="data.subdivisions.iso_only:int">203</stat> ISO-only subdivisions never appear directly as a city's subdivision.

A city's `name` is GeoNames' primary name, or GeoNames' ASCII name where the primary name is written in a script other than Latin, which applies to <stat key="data.cities.ascii_names:int">24</stat> cities; alternate city names are not shipped.

## Known limitations

Where ISO and GeoNames each ship their own view of the same territory (Kosovo, County Dublin, units ISO hasn't retired yet), that is by design rather than an error; see [Where ISO and GeoNames disagree](#where-iso-and-geonames-disagree).

GeoNames often records a city-level unit twice: a level-1 record and a level-2 child with the same name. Examples include Taipei, Gaborone, Kinshasa, Yamoussoukro, Oslo, Asunción, Wiltz, and Riga and five other Latvian state cities. ISO's code merges into the level-1 record, matching its own level, and the child ships as a GeoNames-only duplicate beneath it.

Kaohsiung (`TW-KHH`) was the one judgment call: its two records have identical metadata (the same Chinese name and Wikipedia link), the level-1 one labelled with the historic name "Takao", and neither says whether it covers the pre-2010 city or the municipality merged with Kaohsiung County that year. It merged into the level-1 record (7280289) for consistency with Taipei, leaving 9613373 as the duplicate.

The <stat key="data.subdivisions.iso_only:int">203</stat> unmerged ISO subdivisions, listed in [unmerged_subdivisions.md](unmerged_subdivisions.md) and regenerated on every ingest, are each a recorded decision with a stated reason, a non-administrative grouping with no GeoNames twin, or a Singapore district. The 2026-10-02 audit's duplicate scan found no GeoNames record for any of them (see [Discovery](#discovery)).

A "no counterpart" decision is not yet re-checked when GeoNames adds records, though: if GeoNames later adds a place that was added as ISO-only, such as a newly created Ugandan district, it ships twice unless Wikidata links the two, which sends the decision back for review. A check that reopens such decisions on every run is planned.

No per-entry audit verdicts are recorded yet. One full-map audit was run after the 2026-10-02 skill run: every resolution was checked for integrity, and every skill merge, every automerge that wasn't an exact name match or had a same-name record elsewhere in its country, every Wikidata merge whose names diverge, and every ISO-only record were reviewed. It found one wrong merge, now corrected. Its verdicts aren't stored, so it doesn't carry forward to future source updates; a fingerprinted, per-entry audit is planned.

Names ship in Latin script only, so a query written in another script, such as "Москва", matches nothing. Translated names are out of scope.

Only <stat key="data.macroregions.historic_placed:int">10</stat> of the <stat key="data.countries.historic:int">31</stat> historic countries have macroregions. Macroregions come from CLDR's territory containment, which places current countries in its main tree and keeps placements for some withdrawn codes under a deprecated status.

A historic country takes only a deprecated placement, never the main tree, because <stat key="data.countries.historic_reusing_current_alpha2:int">5</stat> historic entries reuse a current country's alpha-2 code (French Afars and Issas, British Antarctic Territory, the Byelorussian SSR, the Gilbert and Ellice Islands and Sikkim would otherwise inherit the macroregions of Anguilla, Bonaire, Belarus, Georgia and Slovakia). A deprecated placement is used only when its code sits in exactly one subregion and belongs to exactly one historic entry; its region is that subregion's parent. That maps the Netherlands Antilles, Burma, East Germany, Metropolitan France, the Neutral Zone, the USSR, East Timor, South Yemen, Yugoslavia and Zaire.

The rest have none: Czechoslovakia and Serbia and Montenegro share the code CS, which CLDR places in Southern Europe, right for Serbia and Montenegro but not for Czechoslovakia, and CLDR places none of the others. The mapped placements are CLDR's conventions rather than historical claims: the USSR sits only in Eastern Europe although it spanned northern Asia, and East Germany sits in Western Europe, following unified Germany's placement.

Historic countries have no currencies or languages. CLDR keys its currency data and territory info by alpha-2 code, and the codes historic entries hold are reused (the same <stat key="data.countries.historic_reusing_current_alpha2:int">5</stat> as for macroregions, plus CS, shared by Czechoslovakia and Serbia and Montenegro), so its entries can't be trusted to describe the withdrawn country. CLDR's `BQ` lists the US dollar of today's Caribbean Netherlands, not the British Antarctic Territory's currency. Withdrawn currencies would also need ISO 4217's historic list, which iso-codes doesn't carry.

A country's language entry has `script` `None` where CLDR's tag names no script, meaning the language in its default script. CLDR's likely-subtags data gives defaults, but both per language (`zh` to Simplified Han) and per territory (`zh-HK` to Traditional Han), and the territory default would label Hong Kong's 5% `zh` entry as Traditional, contradicting its separate 95% `zh_Hant` entry. Until a rule reconciles the two, the script is left unstated rather than inferred.

TR35 describes CLDR's language data, the source of `Language.scripts`, as "used for consistency checking and testing" rather than as reference data. It ships because CLDR is the reference for locale data across widely deployed software and is the only language-to-script source among localis's sources, and it agrees with CLDR's territory info, as above. Its coverage is CLDR's: languages it omits have no scripts, including seven with an official status somewhere (six Guatemalan Mayan languages, among them Kaqchikel and Ixil, and Senegal's Saafi-Saafi), which appear in their country's languages with no scripts of their own.

### 2026-10-04

Text normalization no longer transliterates. Every name and query had passed through `unidecode`, which romanizes every script to ASCII. A license review found it is GPL-2.0-or-later, which would pass obligations on to anyone distributing an application built on localis, and measuring what it actually did left little reason to keep it.

Accent stripping already handled accented Latin; its remaining work was romanizing non-Latin names, 19,882 of the 163,809 subdivision name values and 24 city names. Every record already had a Latin name, so those romanizations never decided whether a Latin query could find a record, and they were lossy: Cairo's Arabic name, written without vowels, became "lqhr@", and Japanese names took Chinese readings.

`normalize()` now folds Latin only. It casefolds, strips accents from Latin letters, spells out the Latin letters the sources contain that Unicode decomposition leaves alone (ß, æ, ø, ł, ı, ə and others), and maps modifier letters and typographic punctuation to the ASCII characters they had become before, so ingest's comparisons of Latin names are unchanged. Other scripts are left as written. Schwa (ə) and turned e (ǝ) are distinct letters that both fold to a, as before; their capitals Ə and Ǝ had normalized to "@" and "3", because the mapping ran before lowercasing, and now fold to a as well.

Names in other scripts were then dropped from the data rather than shipped unfolded, since a non-Latin alias could only be found by a query in its own script, which the planned localized names will serve properly. Subdivision alternate names are now limited to Latin script, and a city whose GeoNames name is in another script takes GeoNames' ASCII name.

The rebuild left two ISO subdivisions unmatched, Minsk (`BY-HM`, "Horad Minsk") and Saint Petersburg (`RU-SPE`, "Sankt-Peterburg"), which had matched GeoNames only through romanized Cyrillic aliases; the resolve-subdivisions skill merged both with their GeoNames records. Dropping the non-Latin aliases also restored 274 Latin aliases that deduplication had discarded as spellings of a Cyrillic one, since it keeps the form with more non-ASCII characters. Subdivisions' shipped data fell from 10,866 to 9,930 KiB, and their fully loaded memory from 47.4 to 41.0 MiB.

Historic countries had taken Wikidata names only through a former alpha-2 that no other entry shared, so an entry whose code ISO reused, such as Czechoslovakia's CS, got none, and only 4 of the 31 carried Wikidata aliases. They're now matched by their ISO 3166-3 alpha-4 code (P773), which every historic entry has on Wikidata, and 25 of the 31 carry aliases: with `include_historic` set, "Czechoslovakia", "Soviet Union" and "Yugoslavia" find their entries. An item that also holds a current alpha-2 is that country's own item, so its names stay with the current country. One name the new items brought, the demonym "Soviets", went on the blocklist.

The Wikidata crosswalk was queried live on every rebuild, written to an ignored file and applied unreviewed, so a Wikidata edit could relink a subdivision with nothing marking the source as changed, and nothing recorded the state a release was built from. The result is now committed and gated as described under [Merge precedence](#merge-precedence). The country names query got the same treatment without the gate, since an alias change has no merge to review and already shows in the ingest PR's diff of `countries.tsv`. A hash in the manifest, the ETag equivalent first planned, was dropped: the committed file already identifies the snapshot, and comparing against it is the change check.

The pipeline had run stage by stage, each skipping its rebuild when its own sources were unchanged and dumping straight into the shipped data. A run that failed after countries dumped, with new ids, but before subdivisions did left subdivisions and cities pointing at stale country ids, and the next local run didn't repair them, since their own sources hadn't changed. Every run now builds every dataset into a staging area, and the build replaces the shipped data only once every stage has finished and its record counts reconcile, with the source manifests and Wikidata results promoted alongside it. The manifests of downloaded sources now record each file's SHA-256 as well as its ETag, since an ETag identifies a version only to the server that issued it. Each run reports the records it added, removed and changed per dataset, compared by key, in `ingest/<stage>/outputs/change_report.md`, which the ingest PR summarizes, and records how it ended in `ingest/pipeline.log`.

The iso-codes files had been fetched from the project's main branch by name, so a manifest's URL didn't identify which version of a file a build used, and its SHA-256 could verify the file but not find it again. Main was nine months ahead of iso-codes' latest release, v4.20.1 (2026-01-01); following releases instead would have rolled back the ISO changes made since and kept localis that far behind ISO, so main is still followed, but each run now looks up main's current commit and fetches every iso-codes file at it, and the manifests record that commit in their URLs. CLDR's files, fetched from its `cldr-json` repository's main branch by name in the same way, are now fetched at its current commit too. A download that ended short of its stated length had also been accepted as complete, since Python's HTTP client doesn't flag it; it now fails and is retried, as are failed downloads, which hadn't been retried at all.

### 2026-10-03

Added macroregions. UNSD's M49 table and CLDR's territory containment were both considered; CLDR was chosen because it is versioned, machine-readable and already a source, and localis claims CLDR's hierarchy rather than M49's, since CLDR flattens M49's intermediate level and adds codes M49 lacks. The name follows the sources too: "continent" appears in neither, and "regions" alone was rejected because Region is one of the most common ISO 3166-2 subdivision types. All six of CLDR's groupings are included rather than a selection, and a grouping's parent is taken from CLDR's own filing of it under a region instead of inferred from the subregions it contains.

A country's region and subregion form a path, kept apart from its groupings, which overlap without nesting. Historic countries were limited to CLDR's deprecated placements after five of them turned out to reuse a current country's alpha-2 code, which would have given them that country's macroregions.

Countries now carry their placements: `place_macroregions()` sets each country's path and groupings during the countries stage and fails the ingest if CLDR leaves a current country unplaced, so a new ISO country that CLDR hasn't placed yet stops the run rather than shipping without a region. A macroregions rebuild now forces a countries rebuild, since country rows store macroregion ids.

ISO 3166-1 numeric codes had been stored as published, as zero-padded strings ("076"), although `Country.numeric` is an integer and historic numerics were already converted. The subdivisions filter index held "076" while the runtime asked for 76, so filtering subdivisions by a country's numeric code as an integer never matched. Numerics are now integers from the start.

### 2026-10-02

Added the margin floor after investigating the known wrong merges: `MG-D` (margin 1) and `TW-TNN` (margin 3) were two of only three automerges within 3 points of their thresholds. `TW-TNN` also showed that automerge's same-level comparison can't find a match ISO and GeoNames file at different levels, so the skill now sees every subdivision in the country, with claimed records marked.

Investigating also found 77 places shipping twice, an ISO-only record beside a GeoNames-only twin of the same name (Bayern and Bavaria, Hamburg, most of Iceland's and Lithuania's municipalities). All 77 came from the skill's 251 "no counterpart" decisions, most likely made before its candidates included alternate names; all 251 were cleared for re-resolution, and declaring "no counterpart" now requires a concrete reason.

The `RU-AL`/`RU-ALT` dispute was a stale skill decision: Barnaul, Altai Krai's capital, sat under Altai Republic, and Wikidata's mapping was right. Merged records also kept GeoNames' parent instead of ISO's, contradicting ISO's authority over hierarchy; they now take ISO's. Ipregistry was dropped as a source.

Because Altai showed an old skill decision can silently outrank a correct Wikidata mapping, skill decisions are now checked against Wikidata on every run, and any unacknowledged disagreement goes back for review. 437 old skill merges that Wikidata independently reproduces were cleared, leaving Wikidata to resolve them and re-check them every run; 0 of the remaining old merges disagreed with Wikidata.

Records the type-family rule rejects are now shown to the skill marked as a type mismatch instead of hidden, after the rule hid Hamburg, Aberdeen City and Glasgow City from it: GeoNames names each as a city while ISO types it a state or council area. Countries with no GeoNames subdivisions at all, Singapore so far, are now added as-is by automerge on every run rather than through permanent skill decisions.

The skill had been merging Hungary's cities with county rights into their járás, a district containing the city plus its surrounding settlements. That relabelled each district as the city and, through the city link, put every village in the district under the city instead of under its county. The 18 such merges were converted to "no counterpart" decisions.

The agent had also been saving rulings and its own conclusions as private rules and applying them in later sessions; those were removed, and the rulings that should stand were written into the skill's instructions as its only accepted reasons for "no counterpart".

Finally, every skill decision without a recorded reason and decider was cleared, 293 of 324, so that each one is resolved again under the current rules and leaves a traceable record. 31 already met that standard. The next ingest left 315 subdivisions for the skill, with Wikidata and automerge resolving the rest under the current gates.

Guinea-Bissau's provinces were added to the non-administrative rule, reversing their earlier exclusion. That exclusion had been inferred from ISO's two-tier structure alone, never verified against the first test, and sources agree the provinces have no political or administrative effect: the country is administered through its 8 regions and the autonomous sector of Bissau. The skill surfaced it by escalating Leste, whose generic name gave its web search nothing to confirm.

The Marshall Islands' Ralik and Ratak chains were added the same way, after the skill escalated Ratak: sources state the chains are a geographic division, not an administrative one, and ISO's nesting of the municipalities under them had kept automerge from matching the municipalities with GeoNames' level-1 records.

After the skill's run, an independent audit of the whole map checked every resolution's integrity, every Wikidata merge whose names diverge, every automerge that wasn't an exact name match or had a same-name record elsewhere in its country, every skill merge, and every ISO-only record against unclaimed GeoNames records for missed duplicates. It found one wrong merge, `LT-54`, which exposed that a non-administrative grouping's GeoNames twin stayed in automerge's pool; groupings now claim their twins (see [Merge precedence](#merge-precedence)), which also removes those twins as GeoNames-only duplicates. `LT-54` was corrected to its own level-2 record by a human decision.

The audit also showed that corroborating one source with another can only ever verify Wikidata merges: automerge and the skill only see codes Wikidata couldn't validly map, so none of their 523 merges has a Wikidata mapping to agree with.

The skill resolved all 315 orphans under the new rules, run on Opus 5.5 after earlier Sonnet 5 runs had left wrong merges and duplicates. Its escalations shaped the rules further:

- Records the directional-word guard rejects are now shown to it, marked, after the guard hid Grenada's only match (`GD-10` "Southern Grenadine Islands" is GeoNames' "Carriacou and Petite Martinique").
- Monaco's quarters and cities with their own administrative status (Hungary's cities with county rights, Thailand's Pattaya) became stated patterns, since each covers many orphans, while one-off cases such as County Dublin stay human decisions.
- A place too new for GeoNames, such as Uganda's districts created since 2016, still always goes to a human.

A GeoNames-only record's level now follows from its parent's, which moved 63 districts in Iraq's Kurdistan governorates and Equatorial Guinea's provinces from level 2, beside their parents, to level 3.

The resulting ingest left no orphans: 4,843 of 5,046 ISO subdivisions (96.0%) are merged with GeoNames, up from 4,751, and the ISO-only records fell from 295 to 203, while the dataset shrank from 51,803 to 51,711 records as duplicates were removed.

The country layer was reviewed against the same standard, and it didn't meet it. Two hard-coded tables, with no source and no record of why, had replaced ISO 3166-1's `name` for 12 countries and its `official_name` for 9, and `common_name`, an addition by the Debian iso-codes project rather than part of ISO 3166-1, had replaced `name` for 10 more and was documented as ISO's. 22 countries were affected, and for 14 of them ISO's own name existed nowhere in the record, so `"Korea, Republic of"` or `"Iran, Islamic Republic of"` found nothing. Taiwan's ISO official name, "Taiwan, Province of China", had been replaced with "Republic of China", a position on ISO 3166's most contested designation that contradicted the neutral handling of the same dispute among subdivisions.

ISO's `name` and `official_name` now ship exactly as published, `official_name` is `None` where ISO gives none, and iso-codes' short names moved to their own `common_name` field, credited to iso-codes. Search previously dropped any candidate whose `name` didn't match before looking at other fields, so it now scores the best of the three name fields, and the `country` filter on subdivisions and cities matches `common_name` too.

The aliases were reviewed next. A curated alias list of 45 names was no longer merged once 35 of them turned out to be supplied by GeoNames or Wikidata anyway; the rest is under review. It had also given Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba the aliases "Netherlands Antilles" and "Dutch Antilles", which belong to a different, historic entry.

Wikidata names had come from `wiki_countries.json`, a hand-made snapshot with no record of how it was built and no refresh; it was replaced by a live query of each country's English label, alternative labels and short names, filtered by the mechanical rules described under [Countries](#countries), with Wikidata's own IOC and FIFA codes used to recognise 33 sports codes listed as names. The rules can't recognise nicknames, demonyms or misspellings, so a blocklist of 46 such names, each with a reason, removes them; it only ever removes names a source supplied.

Aliases were also never deduplicated: the same name arrived from up to three sources, and Saint Barthélemy carried about 20 spellings of a handful of names. They are now compared by a key that ignores accents, punctuation and common abbreviations, keeping the fullest form of each. After all of this, the 250 current countries ship 576 aliases, each from GeoNames or Wikidata, and 11 carry a `common_name`.

Restructured this document to cover all three datasets, not just subdivisions, and to separate the rules and evidence from the history of how they were reached. Added summary tables, source licensing, source snapshot provenance, country and city methodology, and the constraints on the resolve-subdivisions skill. Corrected an earlier claim that `MG-D` and `TW-TNN` could be fixed with an `audited` entry. `audited` only confirms results and evicts entries that disagree, so the fix has to be a `skill_decisions` decision.

### 2026-10-01

ISO entries at levels 2 and 3 previously competed in separate automerge buckets, even though both draw on GeoNames' level-2 data, which left every third-level entry unmatchable. They now share a bucket. The skill was rewired to use the pipeline's own `candidate_pool()` and `score_candidates()` instead of a separately maintained ranking, which fixed two gaps found in the process: its candidates lacked alternate-name enrichment, and GeoNames targets already claimed by recorded resolutions still appeared as candidates. `ambiguity` orphans now carry their contested GeoNames targets. Estonian `vald` and `linn` were added to the type-family word lists, since GeoNames' Estonian names append them.

`admin_level` became a recursive walk of ISO's full parent chain instead of a one-level check capped at 2, resolving France's third level without a special case. Ireland, Iceland, Lithuania and Malawi were added to the non-administrative rule, Malawi after research found an explicit statement that its regions exist only to group districts. The Philippines was ruled out on closer inspection, because its regions have real administrative function.

The Wikidata crosswalk was added ahead of automerge and resolved roughly 3,861 subdivisions in its first run, cutting automerge's workload by about 90% and orphans by about 60%. Before it went live, every match was checked against the pipeline's existing decisions (4,134 of 4,147 agreed), and a random sample of 25 resolutions only Wikidata could make came back 25 of 25 correct. Checking all 144 disagreements surfaced the populated-place pattern behind 142 of them. The remaining dispute, `RU-AL`/`RU-ALT`, had already surfaced independently in three earlier checks: the type-family disqualification work, the lowest-margin audit, and an earlier crosswalk comparison.

After a full skill and ingest run, 295 of 5,046 ISO subdivisions remained unmerged. The list is published in `unmerged_subdivisions.md` and regenerated on every ingest.

### 2026-09-30

`resolution_map.json` was redesigned into the single record of every subdivision's resolution, with self-reconciling `audited` entries.

Fuzzy scoring previously gave single-token names an extra 5-point discount on top of the length discount. Auditing the lowest-margin merges found confirmed false positives sitting exactly on the resulting threshold: `GB-ENF` Enfield matched Wakefield at exactly 75 against a threshold of exactly 75, two unrelated places sharing only a "-field" suffix, and `GB-EAL` Ealing/Reading, `PL-16` Opolskie/Podlasie, `VN-31` and `VN-32` showed the same knife-edge pattern.

Removing the discount pushed about 16 correct transliteration and grammatical-variant matches (Panjshayr/Panjshir, Kordestān/Kurdistan, several Russian oblasts' case endings) into orphan status in exchange for closing 5 to 7 confirmed false positives. The trade was taken deliberately. Auditing the new lowest-margin merges then found `TW-TNN` and `MG-D`, both still uncorrected.

Type-family disqualification was introduced after `UA-30` Kyiv, the city, automerged with Kyiv Oblast's GeoNames record on a near-perfect bare-name score, while `UA-32`, the actual oblast, never scored well enough to compete. Vilnius (`LT-58`) and Altai showed the same pattern.

A finer-grained version with separate province, department, county and region families was tried first and reverted, after it wrongly rejected nearly every Romanian and Swedish match: GeoNames names Romanian counties "Vâlcea County" while ISO types them "Department", and Sweden's "län" landed in the wrong family. Collapsing to city against area removed 150 wrongly orphaned entries.

`token_sort_ratio` replaced `token_set_ratio` after subset matches merged different places, and ISO names with trailing bracketed content are now split at load time, after `"Stockholms län [SE-01]"` normalized three junk tokens away from GeoNames' "Stockholms".

Initial methodology documented, covering alternate-name enrichment, the scorer change, the ambiguity threshold, type-family disqualification, bracket splitting, and the non-administrative exception for Indonesia, the Dominican Republic and Cabo Verde.
